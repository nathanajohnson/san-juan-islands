Geography

Where land and sea tangle

Nestled in the rain shadow of the Olympics, the San Juans sit between Vancouver Island and mainland Washington — an archipelago of forests, prairies, and tidal labyrinths inside the Salish Sea.

Rain shadow

The Olympic Mountains wring moisture from Pacific storms. Friday Harbor receives roughly half the rain of Seattle — making Garry oak meadows and madrona bluffs possible.

Salish Sea

Recognized by the U.S. (2009) and Canada (2010), the Salish Sea unites Puget Sound, the Strait of Georgia, and the Strait of Juan de Fuca — a single inland sea of shared waters and peoples.

No bridges

Everything arrives by ferry, seaplane, or private boat. Washington State Ferries serve Lopez, Shaw, Orcas, and San Juan from Anacortes — the islands’ living arteries.

History

Deep time to a pig war

Coast Salish peoples have lived with these tides for thousands of years. Empires later drew lines through channels they barely knew — until a single shot nearly made history absurd.

1859 · San Juan Island

The Pig War

When American settler Lyman Cutlar shot a British Hudson’s Bay Company pig rooting in his potato patch, two empires nearly went to war over who owned the San Juans. Soldiers camped at opposite ends of the island for 12 years — American Camp and English Camp — without firing another shot. In 1872, Kaiser Wilhelm I of Germany arbitrated: the border would run through Haro Strait. The islands became American. The pig, history’s most consequential casualty, remains the only fatality.

Whale traffic

The residents of these waters

Two cultures of killer whale share the Salish Sea: fish-eating Southern Residents — critically endangered, tightly bound family pods — and thriving mammal-eating Bigg’s (transient) orcas. Press play to watch a year’s traffic through these channels.

J

J Pod

Southern Resident · ~25 whales

Often the most frequently sighted of the three Southern Resident pods in inland waters. Led through matrilines — grandmothers and mothers hold ecological knowledge of salmon runs.

K

K Pod

Southern Resident · ~16 whales

Smallest of the three pods. Like all Southern Residents, they specialize almost entirely on Chinook salmon — a diet that has become a crisis as runs decline.

L

L Pod

Southern Resident · ~32 whales

Largest Southern Resident pod. They range widely, sometimes remaining offshore for longer stretches, then sweeping into the San Juans when salmon pulse through Haro Strait.

B

Bigg’s (Transients)

Mammal-eaters · thriving

Culturally and genetically distinct from Residents. They hunt seals, sea lions, and porpoises in quiet, stealthy groups. Population is increasing — a rare bright line in marine mammal conservation.

Lime Kiln Point

On the west shore of San Juan Island, Haro Strait runs deep and close to land. Lime Kiln Point State Park — still home to an active 1919 light and historic lime kilns — is one of the finest shore-based orca lookouts on the planet.

    Tidepool etiquette

    👀

    Look, don’t poke

    Hands and tools crush fragile animals and can tear anemones from rock. Observe with eyes and cameras.

    🦶

    Watch your step

    Walk on bare rock when possible. Stepping on seaweed and mussel beds smashes homes you cannot see.

    🪨

    Turn, then return

    If you lift a rock, put it back exactly as you found it — dark underside down — so residents keep their shelter.

    ⏳

    Mind the tide

    Minus tides are best and brief. Know the schedule, keep an eye on the water, and never get cut off by a rising flood.
